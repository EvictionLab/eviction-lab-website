Nayantara is a Research Specialist at the Eviction Lab. At the Eviction Lab, Nayantara supports research examining how poor families interact with child protective service agencies, with a focus on how these systems shape family stability, child welfare, and broader experiences of poverty.
Nayantara is especially interested in how low-income families navigate government services and bureaucracies, and how the design of government systems – in terms of data collection, institutional capacity, and administrative law – can further entrench or lessen the experience of poverty. She completed her undergraduate degree at Trinity College, studying Political Science and Urban Studies. Among other research experiences at Trinity, she conducted research on how sparse migration data and weak state responses limited school access for migrant workers’ children. Based on this research, she partnered with the Karnataka Department of Women and Child Development to design and implement an intervention addressing barriers to educational attainment. Prior to joining the Eviction Lab, Nayantara was a Research Specialist at the University of Pennsylvania. Here, she contributed to research on the psychosocial determinants and downstream effects of poverty in the United States. In her spare time, you can find her biking along the Schuylkill, playing the guitar with her band Catfish Dog, and attempting (poorly) new recipes.
